## where the fees went

Every trade pays its coin's own rate of 1%, 3%, 5% or 10%, chosen at launch, in whatever that pool is priced in, ether or USDG. Of it, the platform keeps 0.3% of the trade at every rate. This is that money, followed to the end, with the stablecoin side carried into ether so the totals add up to something.

These shares are set on the splitter and can be changed, and they have been. What does not change is that half of the platform's cut buys CASHCAT. Only the split between burning it and holding it moves.

Both of those lanes are the same purchase: ether goes to the market and CASHCAT comes back, so both are buying whatever happens afterwards. What the treasury holds is never sold. It pays for listings, exchange partners and the work of putting the token in front of people. A community takeover has to fund that from somewhere, and cannot fund it by selling the thing it is trying to grow.

the two CASHCAT buys are public functions that pay whoever calls them, so the conversions are driven by anyone who wants the fee rather than by us.
