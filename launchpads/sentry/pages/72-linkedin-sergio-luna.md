Six months ago, I wrote a line on here that still anchors everything I am building: liquidity is math, not money. The vocabulary I used then leaned crypto-native: onchain markets, go-to-market rails. That was just the language the moment had. At the time, the idea that tokenization would reshape mainstream finance was not yet as loud of a conversation it is now. But the thesis underneath was never about one kind of asset. It was about how markets themselves are formed. Traditional finance assumes a real market needs deep pools of cash, bankers, and market makers on the sidelines. The insight was that onchain, the rules of a market can be encoded and described mathematically, so liquidity becomes something you deploy, not a pile of capital you have to raise and park. The money a team raises can stay on runway, product, hiring, and compliance, instead of being sacrificed just to make a market exist. Days after I posted it, [BlackRock](https://www.linkedin.com/company/blackrock?trk=public_profile__posts-text)'s [Larry Fink](https://www.linkedin.com/in/laurencefink?trk=public_profile__posts-text), writing in [The Economist](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/the-economist?trk=public_profile__posts-text), put the broader shift plainly: "tokenisation today is roughly where the internet was in 1996." I said then that Mavrk exists for this moment, and I meant it. When the largest asset manager in the world is arguing that this modernizes market infrastructure, the question is no longer whether markets move onchain. It is who builds the rails, and who they are built for. That second part is the whole point for me. The vision has not changed. The world around it has caught up, and the vision I have been building toward from the start is not a faster market for the same insiders. It is open access to capital markets for the companies the current system was never built to serve: the capital-constrained founder, the profitable small business, the company with real traction but not a decade of private rounds or millions to spend on a traditional IPO. Same thesis I wrote down in November. A much bigger moment to build it in. Liquidity is Math, not Money. Most people still think about “going to market” the old way: raise capital → hire intermediaries → let someone else decide how and when your asset trades. In traditional finance, liquidity comes from large pools of cash, bankers, and market makers. The assumption is that deep markets only exist if a lot of money is parked on the sidelines. Onchain markets let us do something different. When markets live on programmable ledgers, the rules of trading are encoded in smart contracts. Price and depth can be described mathematically, not just by how much cash sits in an account. A simple analogy: it’s like running an order book entirely onchain in a transparent, mathematically sound environment, where the rules are open for anyone to inspect. That’s the core ethos behind what I’m building with Mavrk: treating liquidity as a computational primitive instead of a capital requirement. Our thesis is that onchain markets will increasingly encode liquidity as programmable math instead of parked capital, and that platforms which let projects launch markets this way will become the default go-to-market rails for crypto-native assets. In practice, a team decides how many “shares” of their network they want to represent as tokens and what reference price reflects their work and traction. From there, they can plug into an onchain market where trading behavior follows clear, open rules—without needing to lock away an equivalent pile of cash just to make the market feel “real.” Any capital they do raise can stay focused on runway, product, hiring, and compliance instead of being sacrificed to seed liquidity. This challenges the usual “raise a big pool, then list” mindset, but if it consistently proves to be fair, transparent, and efficient, I believe it can become a new default for how projects come to market. This isn’t legal advice and it’s not about telling anyone how to raise money or what is or isn’t a security. Mavrk is infrastructure for onchain markets; teams still need their own legal and compliance guidance. If you’re interested in market mechanics, liquidity design, or how this kind of tooling might fit into the broader crypto capital markets stack, I’m always open to compare notes.
