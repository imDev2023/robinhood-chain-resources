Summary

We propose establishing the Virtuals Foundation (the “Foundation”) which is a non-profit foundation dedicated to promoting our ecosystem's growth, adoption and technological advancement which can support incubation programs, strategic partnerships, grant programmes and talent acquisition. This proposal will include incorporating the Foundation (details below) and then allocating the DAO treasury to the Foundation to fulfil its mission of positioning our protocol at the forefront of AI innovation.

Background and Implementation

Virtuals Protocol was developed by a group of core contributors with limited advice in relation to legal structuring and has previously operated in a fairly informal manner. Virtuals Protocol has now grown to a point whereby it is sensible for the long-term benefit of the ecosystem that a more robust legal structuring is necessary to ensure that participants in the ecosystem are better protected from liability and bad behavior.

This will be implemented by the incorporation of a Cayman foundation company, appointing a suitable independent professional director for the Cayman foundation company and subsequently contributing the treasury assets into the Foundation. The Foundation will have no shareholders or beneficiaries, existing for the sole purpose of furthering the Virtuals ecosystem and core contributors will continue to be on the treasury multisigs. There will be no changes to the governance process (i.e. voting on proposals etc) from a tokenholder perspective. For the avoidance of doubt, this restructuring does not affect the legal status of our team members, and they will continue to be employed under our current operating entity without any changes to their existing contractual arrangements.

Reasons for setting-up Foundation

- A legal entity shields DAO participants from exposure to liability

Virtuals has previously operated as an unincorporated DAO which has no legal form with which it can interact with the traditional legal infrastructure. Tokenholders vote on proposals and core contributors on the multisigs put into place proposals which have been voted on favorably by the tokenholders.

Virtuals has grown significantly in the last few months and this sort of unincorporated structure could create liability for tokenholders especially as the DAO begins entering into higher value strategic partnerships and programs to further the Virtuals ecosystem.

Significantly, US courts have very recently characterised DAOs as general partnerships (https://decrypt.co/292275/california-court-rules-lido-dao-members-can-be-held-liable-under-partnership-laws) which exposes core contributors and persons deemed to be members of the DAO to various risks such as being personally liable for decisions, debts and obligations of the DAO, liability for other members’ actions, etc. The incorporation of a foundation as a separate legal entity is meant to address this risk.

- A Foundation protects the treasury from bad actors

In the absence of a foundation to steward the Virtual’s treasury, the Virtual’s ecosystem has no legal recourse to any of the core contributors or anyone who might subsequently be on the Virtuals Protocol’s multisig for bad behavior.

Through the incorporation of a Foundation, we ensure that the assets of the Virtual’s treasury are held by a specific legal entity, and legally earmarked for ecosystem development and growth, as opposed to ‘trust me bro’.

- A Foundation is in line with industry best practice

Many leading blockchain projects and protocols opt to have a Cayman foundation structure to support the growth of the relevant ecosystems. Cayman law creates significant safeguards on how the assets of a foundation can be used and thus the foundation structure is best-suited for the long-term governance of the Virtuals Protocol’s ecosystem.

Foundation Structure & Governance

The Foundation will act as an independent entity working in parallel with the DAO. The Foundation’s charter will specifically state that its purpose is to promote and support the development of the Virtuals ecosystem. To ensure transparency, the Foundation will report to the DAO regularly on milestone achievements. The Foundation will also consult with the community in making major decisions via tokenholder vote. Lastly, allocation of resources by the Foundation will be made transparent to members of the DAO by means deemed reasonable by the Foundation.

Conclusion

We believe that setting up the Foundation will provide the necessary infrastructure for Virtuals to engage in its Mission and Objectives so that Virtuals will be primed to be a leading force in AI innovation.

Ethermage here. Addressing some of the initial comments from CT here:

1. Are other legal forms (DUNAs, BORGs, plain operating companies) viable or preferable for a $1 billion AI protocol?
The Cayman Foundation structure is presently the gold standard for protocol structuring globally and has attained this status due to its neutrality, flexibility, and global acceptance. Unlike traditional companies, it has no shareholders or outright owners and exists purely for the benefit of the protocol. Thus, it aligns with the governance decisions of the DAO (except for in exceptional circumstances), thereby maintaining the objective of decentralization. It offers a separate legal personality to (a) shield DAO participants from direct personal liability, (b) hold treasury assets (including tokens) in a transparent and accountable manner (while being optimised for tax purposes), and (c) execute contracts and establish legal relationships with third parties on behalf of the DAO — all through a structure that is based in a jurisdiction that is tax-neutral, crypto-forward, and well-tested within the crypto industry.
In addition, the Cayman Islands is a tax neutral jurisdiction and Cayman Foundations are not subject to direct taxes (such as capital gains tax, income tax etc…) and this is a favourable situation to be in given the potentially high value of the treasury assets that is intended to hold and manage as well as the transactions that the Cayman Foundation may need to engage in. US entities can face massive tax obligations by virtue of being under the tax jurisdiction of the US, and this can impose an overly large financial burden which .
While alternatives like the DUNA (Decentralized Unincorporated Nonprofit Association) were considered as well, the fact that these (a) are largely untested, (b) require ongoing compliance with U.S. laws , and (c) are going to be subject to US taxation requirements, has led us to decide that the Cayman Foundation offers a better alternative for a globally distributed DAO such as the Virtuals DAO.

2. How will we manage the downsides of a Cayman Foundation Structure?
To address potential downsides such as regulatory exposure, incentive misalignment, governance drift, or administrative cumbersome processes and overhead, the team will work together with professional advisors (legal, tax and other advisors) to:
Draft and implement clear constitutional documents and DAO resolutions, ensuring alignment between the Foundation Director/Supervisor and DAO votes;
Appointing crypto-native directors, whose experience and industry reputation hangs on making efficient decisions for the protocol v.s. Directors with no skin in the game.
Transparent, community-approved governance procedures, including defined roles for signers, advisors, and service providers;
Limit regulated activity (e.g., avoid conducting retail token sales directly from the Foundation);;
Engage experienced and crypto-native Cayman service providers to meet compliance, AML, and economic substance requirements;
Maintain transparency through public reporting and regular updates to token holders.
These measures ensure the Cayman Foundation remains compliant, accountable, and aligned with the DAO’s decentralised ethos.

3. Would a lean “dev‑co‑only” model—multi‑year token distribution to the company, no formal DAO rights—run the protocol more efficiently?
We ensure operational efficiency by granting DevCo the authority to manage the non-native token treasury for hiring and operational expenses, without requiring a governance proposal. Governance is only triggered when actions involve the $VIRTUAL tokens in the treasury.

4. How will the structure remain flexible when U.S. securities regulation becomes clearer (6‑24 month horizon)?
Should it become necessary to adjust the structure in response to evolving U.S. securities regulatory considerations—or to capitalize on a more favorable U.S. regulatory environment, if such conditions arise—the current framework allows for flexibility. This may include the incorporation of alternative entities and the orderly transfer of assets, contractual relationships, and accounts from the Foundation Company to the new structure, ensuring continuity of operations and compliance.

5. Who appoints the “independent” foundation director(s) and what qualifications are required?
The initial independent director of the Foundation will be appointed by the Virtuals team, based on demonstrated credentials and familiarity with the crypto and Web3 ecosystem. Key selection criteria include relevant industry experience, references from reputable projects, and a clear record of independence. In the future, the DAO may participate in or assume responsibility for the selection and appointment process to further decentralize governance.

6. How can tokenholders remove or replace a director who drifts from the mission or acts against community interests?
The governance framework of the Foundation is designed to reflect and uphold the will of the DAO. If a director is perceived to be acting inconsistently with the DAO’s objectives or community interests, tokenholders may initiate a DAO proposal to remove and replace that director.
Subject to the Foundation’s governing documents (such as the Articles of Association and Charter), a successful DAO vote to remove a director will be treated as a binding resolution. Upon the passing of such a resolution, the necessary steps will be taken by the Foundation to formally execute the removal and appoint a replacement director in accordance with applicable Cayman law.
This mechanism ensures that the Foundation remains accountable to the DAO, and that governance authority can be meaningfully exercised by the community to uphold the mission and integrity of the project.

7. What safeguards prevent low‑engagement directors from blocking growth or awarding themselves excessive pay?
Under Cayman law, directors of a Foundation may receive reasonable compensation; however, this is subject to the terms of a pre-negotiated appointment agreement, which is reviewed and approved prior to formal appointment. Compensation levels are set transparently and reflect the director’s responsibilities, scope of engagement, and market standards.
Importantly, Cayman Foundation directors are fiduciaries and are legally prohibited from distributing Foundation assets to themselves or otherwise using Foundation resources for personal gain. Any misconduct or failure to act in the best interests of the Foundation or the DAO community would constitute a breach of fiduciary duty and may result in removal and potential legal liability. These structural safeguards help ensure that directors act in alignment with the mission and governance expectations of the DAO.

8. How will director incentives remain aligned with core contributors, dev co, and tokenholders over time?
The following multi-layered approach will help to ensure that the director’s incentives remain closely aligned with those of core contributors, Dev Co, and tokenholders while the director remains appointed for the Cayman Foundation.
Statutory Fiduciary Duties
Cayman law obliges Foundation directors to act in good faith and in the best interests of the Foundation. This legal fiduciary standard supersedes personal interests and binds the director to uphold decisions validly adopted by the DAO, except where doing so would be unlawful, commercially unreasonable, or expose the Foundation to unwarranted liability.

Fixed Contractual and Compensation Framework
Pre-negotiated appointment agreement: The director’s remuneration is fixed in advance, benchmarked against market rates, and subject to periodic review by a DAO-mandated oversight committee.

No discretionary payouts: Directors cannot unilaterally adjust their own compensation or distribute Foundation assets to themselves.

Performance-based claw-backs: Material breaches of duty or sustained under-performance trigger removal procedures.

Reputational and Industry Accountability
Independent directors are publicly disclosed (“doxxed”) senior professionals whose reputations and future mandates depend on maintaining credibility with leading projects, service providers, and regulators. Misalignment with the DAO would damage their standing and career prospects.

DAO Oversight & Removal Rights
Governance documents empower tokenholders to propose and vote on a director’s removal at any time. A successful DAO resolution is binding, ensuring directors remain responsive to evolving community priorities.

Conflict-of-Interest Protocol
Any real or perceived conflict—financial, professional, or relational—must be promptly disclosed. The director must abstain from deliberations where independence could be compromised, safeguarding objectivity in decisions affecting Dev Co or individual contributors.

9. Which decisions stay on‑chain (DAO vote) and which migrate to the Foundation’s off‑chain discretion?
High level decisions related to the usage of the treasury (e.g. allocating 10% of tokens for ecosystem growth initiatives) and the $VIRTUAL token (e.g. tokenomics) will go through a DAO vote, while day-to-day operations for the Foundation will be carried out off chain (e.g. working with legal counsel).

10. When, if ever, can the Foundation veto a DAO‑approved proposal?
Cayman law obliges Foundation directors to act in good faith and in the best interests of the Foundation. This legal fiduciary standard supersedes personal interests and binds the director to uphold decisions validly adopted by the DAO, except where doing so would be unlawful, commercially unreasonable, or expose the Foundation to unwarranted liability.

11. Can governance rights be minimized to the smallest defensible set while retaining decentralization claims?
As a principle, all product-level decisions (e.g. launchpad features, points, etc.) are at the full discretion of the core contributors. DAO governance is applicable for decisions relating to the treasury and the $VIRTUAL token.

12. Does the entire treasury move to the Foundation at once, or in KPI‑linked tranches?
The treasury will be under the mandate of the Foundation, but the core contributors will remain in control of the treasury multisig.

13. What legal and technical measures protect the treasury from misappropriation or “foundation coups”?
The treasury is secured by an institutional-grade multi-signature wallet. All multi-sig signers will be required to enter into a legally binding agreement that clearly defines their roles, responsibilities, and fiduciary obligations. This agreement will establish personal and contractual liability in the event of misconduct, negligence, or any breach of duty, thereby reinforcing accountability and alignment with the DAO’s governance framework.

14. How are core contributors retained on multisig wallets without hampering the Foundation’s independence?
There are sufficient independent signatories to prevent bad actors from attempting a takeover of a multisig. In addition, having a multisig agreement will also create legally binding duties and obligations to manage the checks and balances required.

15. If the dev co eventually receives additional tokens for operational agility, what vesting or milestone terms apply?
Any additional tokens allocated from the treasury will need to undergo and pass a DAO proposal. The specific vesting schedule or milestones will be defined on a case-by-case basis in the proposal.

16. Will the full Foundation charter and bylaws be published before the vote?
To facilitate timely formation and operational readiness, the Foundation will initially be incorporated using a standard set of constitutional documents. Following incorporation, a comprehensive governance charter and bylaws will be prepared by professional legal advisors and subsequently submitted to the DAO for review and approval by community vote prior to formal adoption. This phased approach balances execution efficiency with the DAO’s commitment to transparent, community-driven governance.

17. What reporting cadence is guaranteed—real‑time on‑chain dashboards, quarterly PDFs, livestream AMAs, or a mix? Which performance metrics (grants issued, partnerships closed, cash burn, runway) will be disclosed? How will the community audit grant outcomes and strategic‑partnership ROI?
The initial focus of establishing the Cayman Foundation is to address pressing legal and structural considerations—most notably, to shield DAO participants from potential personal liability and provide a compliant and durable framework for future operations. This foundational step enables us to move forward with greater legal and operational clarity.
Once the Cayman Foundation is formed, the operational governance framework, including the cadence, format, and scope of reporting will be defined. These governance processes will be determined with input from the community, and guided by what is commercially reasonable, legally compliant, and aligned with best practices in the industry.

18. Can the current team manage both DAO processes and Foundation obligations without slowing product delivery?
The core team will remain focused on building products, while the Foundation will be overseen by the independent director with legal counsel and other professionals engaged as required.

19. If extra staffing is needed, will the Foundation cover community managers, legal counsel, and ops roles?
Yes, additional resources will be employed by the Foundation where needed and will be made transparent in a reasonable manner to members of the DAO.

20. How will role overlap or redundancy between dev co and Foundation be avoided to prevent “multiple competing groups with complex structures”?
The roles of the DevCo and the Foundation are clearly delineated - the DevCo oversees all product decisions and development, while the Foundation oversees governance relating to the treasury and the $VIRTUAL token.

21. To what extent does the Cayman foundation shield tokenholders and contributors from U.S. partnership liability?
The establishment of a Cayman Foundation as the legal wrapper for the DAO is specifically intended to mitigate the risk of the DAO being treated as an unincorporated association or general partnership under U.S. law—a scenario which could potentially expose tokenholders and contributors to joint and several liability.
Unlike general partnerships, which generally arise or may be implied/deemed by default when two or more persons carry on a business for profit without forming a legal entity, the Cayman Foundation is a separate legal personality with no shareholders or members. It serves as a formal entity that can hold assets, enter into contracts, and assume legal obligations on behalf of the DAO. By centralizing the DAO’s legal relationships, treasury, and operations within the Cayman Foundation, the structure significantly reduces the likelihood that the DAO could be characterized as a de facto U.S. partnership.
While no structure can completely eliminate risk—particularly in the context of evolving U.S. regulatory interpretations—the Cayman Foundation is well-tested within the crypto industry and provides a clear legal separation between the DAO’s activities and its tokenholders or contributors. This reduces the exposure of individuals to unintended liability and enhances the DAO’s defensibility in the event of legal or regulatory scrutiny.
Accordingly, the adoption of a Cayman Foundation not only supports decentralised governance but also acts as a critical layer of liability protection for community participants.

22. How quickly can the structure pivot if regulators soften their stance or create a clearer token taxonomy?
Should it become necessary to adjust the structure in response to evolving U.S. securities regulatory considerations—or to capitalize on a more favorable U.S. regulatory environment, if such conditions arise—the current framework allows for flexibility. This may include the incorporation of alternative entities and the orderly transfer of assets, contractual relationships, and accounts from the Foundation Company to the new structure, ensuring continuity of operations and compliance.

23. What precedent exists for unwinding a foundation once a superior legal framework becomes available?
There is growing precedent in the Web3 space for projects evolving or transitioning their legal structures in response to regulatory developments or jurisdictional improvements. While the unwinding of a foundation entity, such as a Cayman Foundation, is relatively uncommon at this stage due to its continued utility and neutrality, the legal framework in Cayman provides clear mechanisms for restructuring, migrating, or dissolving the entity if needed.
Projects that have undergone restructurings—whether to migrate to jurisdictions with more DAO-specific legislation, or to transition from a foundation-based model to a more modular or hybrid structure—have typically done so by:
Establishing a successor entity in the new jurisdiction;
Transferring assets, IP, and contractual relationships to that entity through board-approved resolutions;
Dissolving or maintaining the original foundation as a legacy or service entity depending on ongoing needs.
The Cayman Foundation structure is inherently flexible and can be voluntarily dissolved by its directors or supervisory body once its obligations are met and liabilities settled. This allows the DAO to remain agile and to migrate to more favourable or purpose-built frameworks (such as a U.S.-recognized DAO entity, should one gain traction) without undue friction.
While formal unwinding of a foundation remains rare due to its robustness, the legal tools and growing industry practice for restructuring or transitioning are well established and available to the DAO should circumstances evolve.

24. Does the foundation model clash with the project’s fast‑moving, founder‑driven culture? How will the team keep decision‑making speed and product focus while satisfying governance optics? In the worst‑case scenario, could bureaucratic drag let centralized competitors out‑innovate Virtuals?

We ensure operational efficiency by granting DevCo the authority to manage the non-native token treasury for hiring and operational expenses, without requiring a governance proposal. Governance is only triggered when actions involve the $VIRTUAL tokens in the treasury.
The fast-moving, founder-driven culture will continue - product decisions will remain at the full discretion of the core contributors, while governance is handled separately by the foundation. Through this structure, decentralization is preserved and the DAO members are shielded from liability while ensuring that the team is able to continue innovating on products.
